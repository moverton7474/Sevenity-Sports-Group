Three industries touch an athlete. None of them connect.

An athlete ends up hiring a trainer, a content person, and eventually an agent — three vendors who never talk to each other, none of whom is responsible for the outcome that matters. Sevenity is built to sit across all three.

How Brand Intelligence works

Follower counts are the worst way to price an athlete.

They are the number every competitor leads with, and the number brands trust least. What actually predicts whether a sponsorship converts is how an audience behaves — and that is measurable.

STEP 01

Read the behavior

We separate audiences into three types. Forwarders privately send content to people they know. Conversationalists argue in the comments. Passive audiences watch and scroll. Each one converts for a completely different kind of sponsor.

STEP 02

Map the pillars

Faith, skill work, comedy, family, style, community. We score what an athlete genuinely posts against a category database of brands that buy that exact audience — so the pitch is never a stretch.

STEP 03

Surface the conflicts

The deal that quietly costs money is the one nobody flagged. Own merch versus an apparel exclusive. School outfitter restrictions. Local sponsors against a national audience. We catch those before a term sheet does.

STEP 04

Price honestly, then go get it

We benchmark off median performance with a bonus tied to the proven ceiling — the structure that survives a brand's audit. Then we work the list: outreach, negotiation, activation, reporting.

The asset nobody was pricing

3,638 sends against 201 comments — an 18:1 ratio. This audience does not discuss his content, it privately forwards it. A send is a one-to-one recommendation from someone you trust: the highest-intent unit on the platform, and almost no brand asks for it in reporting.

What changed as a result

Comment-driven activations were taken off the table — no tag-a-friend contests, no giveaways. The target list was rebuilt around one-tap, nationally available offers, led by a category with real budget that nobody was competing for. A conflict with his own merch line was flagged before any apparel exclusive was signed.

Choosing which metric a contract reports on is choosing the rate. That decision is the entire job.
